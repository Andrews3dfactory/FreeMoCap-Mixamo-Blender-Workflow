Common Mistakes

Small setup and capture choices can save a lot of rework. Use these checks when a take or import does not behave as expected.

7 checks·Beginner friendly

Software changes between releases. Treat these as practical checks, not strict requirements for every FreeMoCap version or capture setup.

FFmpeg is not in PATH

Installing FFmpeg does not automatically make the ffmpeg command available. Add the directory that contains ffmpeg.exe (commonly C:\ffmpeg\bin) to your user PATH, then close and reopen Command Prompt. Verify with where ffmpeg.

Follow the FFmpeg PATH setup guide if the command is still missing.

Importing .3GP directly without checking support

Do not assume every installed release can read every phone recording. Try the original video first; if import fails or the app documents a required format, convert a copy to MP4 and keep the original unchanged.

See video conversion for a sample FFmpeg command and format checks.

Recording at very low resolution

Low-resolution footage can make body landmarks difficult to distinguish, especially when the subject is far from the camera. Frame the full body while keeping it large enough to see, and use the highest practical recording quality for your device and lighting.

There is no single resolution threshold that guarantees good tracking; clear, well-framed footage matters too.

Using a 10 FPS recording

A 10 FPS clip can miss fast movement and look choppy after reconstruction. Prefer a stable, higher frame rate supported by your phone and workflow—30 FPS is a practical starting point—then confirm how your installed tools interpret timestamps and frame rate.

Skipping calibration when the workflow requires it

Multi-camera reconstruction or a specific world coordinate setup may depend on calibration. Follow the guidance for your camera arrangement and installed release. Calibration is not required for every single-camera test, and a mismatched board setup can make results worse.

Read the calibration guide before printing or recording a board.

Letting the body leave the frame

When hands, feet, or the head move outside the image, the tracker has no pixels to recover. Leave room around the entire movement, test the widest pose, and keep the camera fixed for the take.

Use the recording guide for framing and camera placement tips.

Recording in poor or changing light

Dim rooms, strong backlight, flicker, and hard shadows can reduce contrast or blur the subject. Use even light, avoid bright windows directly behind the performer, and keep exposure consistent where your camera allows it.
