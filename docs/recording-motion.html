Recording motion

Get a clean iPhone take with a steady camera, full-body framing, and enough image detail for tracking.

iPhone capture·Start with a short test

Use the original iPhone video whenever possible. Avoid heavily compressed copies. Re-encoding a low-quality source does not restore lost detail.

Choose recording quality

  • A practical target is 1080p or higher at 30–60 FPS when supported by your phone and appropriate for available storage.
  • 4K is optional, not essential for every workflow. Higher resolution increases storage and processing requirements.
  • Avoid very low-resolution sources (such as 176 × 144) and low frame rates (such as 10 FPS) when you can record a better source.
  • Keep the original file and transfer it without messaging-app recompression when possible.

Set up the camera

  1. Place the iPhone on a stable tripod or mount. Keep the camera still; avoid panning, zooming, and changing orientation mid-take.
  2. Frame the full body from head to feet, with some space around the hands and limbs for the whole movement.
  3. Use bright, even lighting and a clear background with contrast from the subject's clothes.
  4. A distance of around 8–15 feet can be a starting point if the room allows, but prioritize clear, full-body framing over a fixed distance.
  5. Reduce motion blur and occlusion. Fitted clothing can make body landmarks easier to see; avoid props or loose fabric hiding joints.

Record a first test

  1. Start recording before entering or moving in the capture area.
  2. Pause briefly, perform a simple movement at a natural speed, then pause before stopping.
  3. Keep the entire action in view. For an initial single-camera test, choose a movement that does not frequently hide limbs behind the torso.
  4. Play the file on the computer and confirm it is sharp, complete, and has the expected orientation and duration.

If you are using multiple cameras

Use the FreeMoCap instructions for your camera setup. Multiple independent iPhone clips are not automatically synchronized. For multi-camera reconstruction, camera timing, overlapping views, and a matching calibration all matter; keep cameras fixed after calibration.

Next step

Import the original clip if your FreeMoCap version supports it, or use an MP4 copy if needed. Continue to FreeMoCap processing.
